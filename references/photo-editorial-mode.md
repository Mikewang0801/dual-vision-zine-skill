# Variant A — Photo-Preserving Editorial Mode

Read this file only when Variant A is selected.

## Artistic Aim

Create a premium editorial poster in which the original photograph remains the factual and emotional anchor. Elevate it through disciplined art direction rather than cosmetic decoration.

“Premium” should be visible as decisive crop or framing, controlled negative space, one clear hierarchy, purposeful typography, one source-derived intervention, coherent material, and restraint.

Do not equate premium quality with black-and-gold palettes, thin serif type, fashion branding, fake magazine mastheads, or arbitrary English words.

## Photo Invariants

Treat the supplied photo as an edit target and immutable photographic anchor.

Preserve unless the user explicitly asks otherwise:

- subject identity and count;
- face, body, pose, expression, and defining accessories;
- place identity and major scene geometry;
- perspective, lighting direction, and meaningful foreground/background relationship;
- truthful photographic texture.

Allow crop, scale, framing, extension into paper, restrained tonal grading, typography, source-derived graphic overlays, paper margins, masks, underprints, lines, fields, illustration fragments, and removal of irrelevant screenshot artifacts.

Avoid repainting the photograph, beautifying faces, replacing clothing, inventing scenery, changing body shape, or smoothing the photo into synthetic AI imagery.

## Choose One Layout Family

- **Full-bleed editorial:** the photo carries the canvas; type occupies genuine quiet space and one restrained intervention redirects the eye.
- **Gallery field:** the photo sits inside generous paper with unequal margins; scale and offset create tension rather than a centered postcard.
- **Directional split:** photo and paper divide along a real road, horizon, gaze, shadow, wall, or architectural edge.
- **Typographic counterfield:** the photo holds one side or layer while a text field counterbalances visual weight.
- **Source-continuation:** one path, branch, garment edge, shadow, window, or silhouette continues beyond the photo as a graphic form.
- **Irregular editorial fragments:** use two or three truthful photo fragments only when the source is already multi-panel or sequence-based.

Do not default to a centered photo with a title beneath it. Avoid equal 50/50 splits unless symmetry is source-driven.

## Graphic Intervention

Use at most one primary intervention and one subordinate echo: source-contour line, opaque cut-paper counterform, translucent or halftone underprint, enlarged source silhouette, restrained broken frame, sparse source-derived illustration, or typographic shape following a source axis.

The intervention must satisfy at least two tests:

- derives contour, position, direction, or rhythm from the source;
- touches or crosses the photo boundary meaningfully;
- changes eye path, balance, figure–ground, or interpretation;
- reinforces a real subject or spatial relationship.

If removing it changes nothing, redesign or omit it. Avoid decorative circles, detached corner blocks, random tape, coordinates, grid fragments, logos, barcodes, issue numbers, and generic magazine furniture.

## Color Direction

Preserve the photograph's natural atmosphere. Add no more than one designed high-chroma hue unless the user supplies a brand palette. Choose it by source resonance, analogous intensification, focused complement, or temperature counterpoint.

Use it as focal pin, counterweight, boundary bridge, directional cue, or typographic emphasis. Natural colors inside the photograph do not count as extra designed hues.

## Copy and Typography

If the user supplies wording, reproduce it exactly. Otherwise author only what the image needs: one concise Chinese phrase, one English word or short phrase, a restrained bilingual pair, or no text.

Typography may be large or small, but its scale must respond to photo weight. Let it align to, cross, hide behind, or reshape a source-derived axis while preserving faces and defining details.

Use one primary type voice and at most one supporting voice. Do not add fake credits, dates, serial numbers, quotations, logos, or publication metadata.

## Prompt Compiler

Compile four compact sections:

1. **Photographic invariants:** what must remain truthful, including identity and scene geometry.
2. **Canvas and hierarchy:** ratio, layout, crop/framing, focal area, negative space, and eye path.
3. **Editorial intervention:** source-derived graphic form, exact color role, copy, typography, material, placement, and photo interaction.
4. **Reproduction and avoids:** tonal treatment, paper or clean editorial finish, exact-text requirement, and highest-risk failures.

Always state:

`Use the supplied image as the edit target and truthful photographic anchor. Preserve the people, identity, face, body, pose, expression, perspective, scene geometry, and natural photographic texture unless the user explicitly requests a change.`

Keep the prompt focused on visible outcomes. Do not request wholesale restyling of the photograph.

## Mode-Specific Hard Avoids

Avoid AI-redrawn faces, altered identity, body reshaping, invented people, changed clothing, replaced scenery, synthetic skin, excessive smoothing, aggressive HDR, fake depth of field, stock-luxury styling, black-and-gold defaults, generic fashion mastheads, arbitrary English, filler metadata, random geometric decoration, crowded typography, unreadable exact copy, logos, CTA, watermarks, glossy mockups, heavy 3D paper, and effects that overpower the source photograph.
