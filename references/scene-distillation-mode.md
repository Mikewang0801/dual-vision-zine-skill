# Variant B — Illustrated Mood Distillation

Read this file only when Variant B is selected.

## Artistic Aim

Turn the source into an independently compelling paper-poster artwork. The source supplies meaning, gesture, relationship, and visual evidence; it does not supply pixels to the final image.

Build through:

```text
source fact → emotional residue → expressive proposition → central tension → visual metaphor → formal embodiment → interpretive opening
```

Use one central tension and one source-derived metaphor. Leave one relationship meaningfully unresolved.

## Distillation

Preserve two to four anchors: semantic nucleus, dominant gesture or path, one key spatial relationship, and one source-specific object, silhouette, material, or repeated motif.

Remove roughly two thirds to nine tenths of descriptive reality. Delete clutter, crowds, redundant objects, realistic background detail, literal foliage texture, and anything that merely proves the source was observed.

Use Semantic likeness for people by default: preserve distinguishing cues such as glasses, hairstyle, garment silhouette, bag, posture, or relative spacing while generalizing facial rendering.

Do not invent unsupported personal history. For multi-panel parallel input, use interval, echo, direction, or shared place as formal relationships unless chronology is established.

## Illustration Grammar

Choose one primary grammar and at most one supporting grammar:

- **Cut-paper mass:** irregular flat shapes carry subjects and weight.
- **Dry-print silhouette:** broad inked masses preserve gesture.
- **Broken contour:** interrupted lines preserve direction and relationship.
- **Rhythm field:** repeated marks compress leaves, crowds, windows, waves, or movement.
- **Fragment stack:** two or three overlapping paper forms establish sequence.
- **Orbit or drift:** selected forms leave a cluster along a source-derived path.

Avoid complete outlines, even detail, realistic shading, polished vector characters, cute cartoon, anime, and children's-book sweetness unless requested.

## Composition

Preserve source-responsive orientation unless the user requests otherwise. Aim for roughly three quarters quiet paper and one active illustration cluster, correcting by visual weight rather than mechanically satisfying percentages.

Choose one family: asymmetric island, torn window, directional drift, rhythmic circulation, staggered fragments, vertical tension, or auxiliary constellation when a real repeatable motif can carry rhythm.

Use asymmetric balance, scale and interval contrast, directional breathing room, and an eye path with entry, focal encounter, movement, and quiet exit. Do not retain the original photographic framing by default.

## Transition Edge

Choose one primary treatment and at most one subordinate treatment: torn-fiber edge, layered grayscale edge, stippled dissolution, irregular source-derived mark edge, or natural isolated contour.

Align it with a real path, gesture, horizon, pressure, material change, or directional break. Keep depth shallow and flat-scanned. Avoid a generic ripped rectangle, sticker outline, fuzzy halo, cast shadow, curled paper, bevel, or using every treatment at once.

## Color Modes

### Standard Accent Mode — default

Keep paper and most illustration inks subdued. Use one unmistakable high-chroma hue as focal pin, counterweight, bridge, directional cue, or rhythmic beat. Choose by source resonance, analogous harmony, temperature counterpoint, or focused complement.

Keep the saturated area perceptually small and purposeful. Use a distributed supporting accent only when the source contains a meaningful repeatable element. Vary scale and intervals; do not create confetti or an even border.

### Solid Color-Block Mode

Activate only with the exact `单色块模式` trigger. Use exactly natural paper tone, one neutral ink system, and one contiguous fully saturated source-derived field.

The field must act as subject, aperture, path, shadow, silhouette, interior, or other central spatial idea. Do not split it into echoes, dots, stripes, or separate objects. Typography may use neutral ink and/or the one hue; no other chromatic color may appear.

Include:

`Color mode: Solid Color-Block Mode. Use exactly one contiguous [exact hue] field. Render every other printed form in neutral charcoal, graphite, warm gray, or off-black ink. Typography may use the neutral ink system and/or [exact hue], but no other chromatic color may appear anywhere.`

## Typography

Text is optional and authorial. Use it only when wording or material presence deepens the proposition, tension, metaphor, or interpretive opening.

It may act as countervoice, interruption, path, field, notation, or quiet trace. User-supplied exact wording must remain correct and legible enough to use. Do not default to captions, generic English fragments, dates, coordinates, issue numbers, or decorative type systems.

## Prompt Compiler

Compile five compact sections:

1. **Expression and visible consequence:** proposition, tension, metaphor, opening, and formal embodiment.
2. **Canvas and attention geometry:** ratio, paper, quiet space, active cluster, hierarchy, and eye path.
3. **Distilled subject and rewrite:** anchors, identity cues, transformations, omissions, and illustration grammar.
4. **Edge, color, and text:** one edge treatment, exact hue and role, color mode, wording, and material behavior.
5. **Reproduction and avoids:** print/scan texture, emotional temperature, no-photo rule, and highest-risk failures.

Always include:

- `Do not reproduce, embed, crop, collage, trace, or retain photographic pixels or photorealistic regions from the reference.`
- `The final image must contain original illustration, paper, and typography only.`

## Mode-Specific Hard Avoids

Avoid original photo fragments, photorealistic regions, photo windows, tracing, rotoscoping, literal full-scene illustration, generic metaphors, random ambiguity, overly faithful anatomy, realistic shading, dense botanical detail, generic dots or grids, unsupported scattering, decorative stamps, tape, multiple bright hues, commercial advertising hierarchy, logos, CTA, glossy mockups, hard shadows, 3D, cinematic lighting, depth of field, neon, fashion-ad drama, and watermarks.
